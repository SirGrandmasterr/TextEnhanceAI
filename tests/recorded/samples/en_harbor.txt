Eliza Marchetti had lived in Port Kellanby for eleven years, and she still could not predict the weather. That morning she recieved a message from the harbor master saying the fog would lift by nine. When the fog finally lifted the boats stayed tied to the pier anyway, because nobody trusted the sky. Yesterday she walks the whole length of the breakwater twice, looking for the loose plank her father had warned her about. In the event that it might possibly rain again, she kept the tarpaulins folded by the door. By noon the gulls had settled on the roof of the fish market, and the town went back to pretending it had never worried at all.
