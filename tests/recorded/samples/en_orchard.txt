Nadia Okonkwo-Reyes bought the land that became Brackenholt Orchard when nobody else wanted it. The soil was thin and the slope faced north, and the first frost of the year always occured a week earlier than in the valley. If the frost came early the whole harvest would be lost, so she learned to read the clouds before she learned the names of her neighbours. Two years ago she plants the last row of saplings by hand, forty of them, in a single afternoon. Due to the fact that the pump had failed, the trees were watered from buckets all through August. This autumn the branches finally bent under the weight of fruit, and she stood at the gate for a long time before she let anyone in.
